# EnronQA deterministic-evaluability audit

Audit question, in Kyle's words: *"go through all the questions to figure out if
they can be rephrased to be deterministically evaluable."*

This report is the aggregate, publishable output. Per-question ledgers (which
quote email text) stay local. Tables referenced as A1–B6 are in
[`AGGREGATES.md`](AGGREGATES.md), regenerated by `04_aggregate.py`. Licensing
findings are in [`LICENSING.md`](LICENSING.md). Counts are in `counts.json`,
`screen_summary.json`, `mcq_summary.json`, `aggregates.json`.

## 1. Scope and coverage (read this first)

reported exactly; nothing in this report extrapolates stage-3 percentages to
the unreviewed remainder as if they were measured.

## 2. Dataset facts (verified from the pinned parquet files)

- Revision `c0b3a9190fd970e83cfbe7d399a08860e43e221e` of `MichaelR207/enron_qa_0922`.
- Questions: train 333,473, dev 105,515, test 89,316, **total 528,304**. This matches Table 3 of the paper exactly.
- Emails: the same **73,772** emails (identical text, identical `path`) appear in all three splits; splits differ only in which questions are attached. The paper's abstract says "103,638 emails"; the released files contain 73,772 unique emails. 30 test rows carry zero questions.
- Every question has exactly one `rephrased_question`, one `gold_answer`, one `alternate_answer` (a second LLM answer that the paper's objectivity filter matched to gold), exactly two `incorrect_answers`, one `gold_rationale`, one `alternate_rationale`, and an `include_email` flag (undocumented in the paper; 1 for 54% of train questions, 0 for every dev and test question).
- Users: 150 in every split. `path` is unique within a split, so `split/path/qidx` is a stable, human-readable record ID; `content_sha1(question + gold)` is also stored.
- Duplicate question *text* occurs in 5,322 groups covering 13,168 questions across splits (e.g. the same signature-block or newsletter question asked of many near-identical emails). In 14.9% of questions the alternate answer is byte-identical to gold after normalization.

## 3. Method

**Stage 1** (`01_enumerate.py`) explodes the nested per-email records into one row per question, asserts list-length consistency and ID uniqueness, and separates the email table so each email is stored once.

**Stage 2** (`02_screen.py`) computes, for every question, regex features on the gold answer (yes/no lead, textual or numeric date, time, money, percent, email address, URL, phone, quoted span, hedge phrases), question-form features (leading wh-word after stripping an "according to …" clause, multi-wh conjunctions), and evidence features against the normalized email text (gold verbatim present; longest verbatim substring of gold as a fraction of gold length; whether all numbers in gold occur in the email; whether capitalized tokens in gold occur in the email). It also screens the two incorrect answers for hedge phrases ("cannot be determined"), post-2002 years, sharing gold's numbers, and near-verbatim email overlap.

**Stage 3** (`03_semantic_review.py`, prompt in `review_prompt.md`) sends one email plus *all* of its questions from all three splits to Claude Fable (`claude-fable-5-1`, effort `low`), several emails per call (median about 42 questions per call). For each item the model returns a schema category, a verdict (`convertible`, `convertible_rewrite` with a rewritten question, or `unconvertible`), a canonical short answer, aliases, flags, and a confidence. Emails are processed in a fixed-seed shuffled order so that any completed prefix is a uniform random sample of emails (and hence of questions in every split and every user). Results are cached per batch; the run is resumable and coverage is counted from the cache.

**Stage 4** (`04_aggregate.py`) joins the ledgers and emits counts only. `05_examples.py` pulls examples for human reading (local). `06_agreement.py` re-runs already-reviewed batches at effort `medium` and measures label agreement.

## 4. Programmatic findings (100% of questions)

Detailed tables: A1–A4.

1. **Gold answers are LLM-written sentences, not spans.** Median gold length is 19 words (p90 = 34, p99 = 55). Only 6.9% of golds occur verbatim in the email, and only 8.1% have at least 80% of their text covered by one verbatim email substring; the median coverage is 25%. Consequently "exact extraction with evidence offsets" is not a description of the current gold; it would be a *new* target that must be constructed per item.
2. **Attribution clauses dominate.** 68.3% of questions contain "according to …". These clauses name the sender or subject and are what makes the questions "specific" in the paper's sense; they are usually not part of the answer and can be kept or dropped in a rewrite without changing the answer schema.
3. **Question form is overwhelmingly "what".** 78.9% "what", 8.9% "who", 6.2% other/leading-prepositional ("on what date", "at what time", "in what context"), 1.3% "why", 1.1% "where", 0.9% "which", 0.6% "when", 0.24% yes/no auxiliaries, 0.6% "how much/many/long/often". Only 0.2% of golds start with "Yes"/"No"; the boolean class is tiny.
4. **Typed surface signals are a minority.** Textual dates appear in 10.2% of golds, numeric dates 1.8%, clock times 5.4%, money 4.6%, percentages 1.5%, email addresses 2.0%, URLs 2.0%, phone numbers 4.0%, quoted spans 11.7%. Together, roughly a third of golds carry at least one typed token; the remaining two thirds are names, things, and explanations.
5. **Evidence support is mostly good but not universal.** 2.7% of golds contain a number that does not occur anywhere in the email (rounding, arithmetic, or hallucination), and 1.5% have fewer than half of their capitalized tokens present in the email. 0.7% of golds contain an explicit hedge ("the email does not specify …", "unclear"), i.e. the gold itself says the information need is not met.
6. **Surface detection is not convertibility.** Table B5 cross-tabulates stage-2 regex hits with stage-3 categories on the reviewed set. A gold that *contains* a date is a `date`-schema item less than half of the time (the date is often context: "the email sent on March 3 says the meeting is about X"). This is why the audit does not treat regex answer-type detection as evidence of semantic convertibility.

Narrative interpretation of the reviewed set follows.

### 5.1 Convertible classes (deterministic scoring is fair after normalization)

- **Named people, organizations, places, email addresses, phone numbers, transaction IDs, document titles** (`person`, `org`, `place`, `email_addr`, `entity`). These dominate the convertible set. The main risk is *aliasing*: emails use "Steven J Kean", "Steve Kean", "Kean" and the header form "Steven J Kean/HOU/EES"; the reviewer emits an alias list and flags `normalization_risk` / `multi_valid_alias` when the email itself varies. A scorer needs case/punctuation/whitespace folding plus the per-item alias list; it should not attempt fuzzy name matching.
- **Dates, times, money, percentages, counts, durations** (`date`, `time`, `datetime`, `money`, `percent`, `number`, `duration`). Canonical forms are ISO dates (with partial dates when the email omits the year), 24-hour times, `USD <amount>`, and unit-bearing numbers. Risks: year inference (the email header supplies the year, the body often does not), time zones (headers are in Pacific or Central time, bodies quote other zones), and approximate quantities ("approximately $5.1 billion").
- **Explicit lists** (`list`). Faithful when the email enumerates the items; the reviewer records whether order carries information. Scoring should be set equality after per-element normalization, with order enforced only when `ordered:true`.
- **A caution on rewrites.** Many `convertible_rewrite` items are two-part questions ("on what date was the letter sent, and what did it request?") where the reviewer keeps the deterministic half and drops the explanatory half. That *narrows* the information need rather than preserving it. The ledger records the rewritten question so this can be audited, and §8 treats a rewrite that drops a sub-question as a partial conversion that must be labelled as such, not silently counted as faithful.
- **Multi-field answers** (`multi_field`). Questions such as "who sent it and on what date" or "what are the positions of A and B" convert to a small JSON object when *each* field is itself deterministic; the reviewer marks items unconvertible when one field is explanatory.
- **Short verbatim spans** (`span`). Faithful when the question asks for a specific phrase that appears in the email ("what did X say the report is not intended to be"). Only a minority of gold answers fall here despite the dataset's RAG framing.

### 5.2 Ambiguous or unconvertible classes

- **Explanatory / purpose / summary questions** (`free_text`): "why", "what is the purpose", "what is the main topic", "what action is X asking Y to take", "what is the context". These are the bulk of `unconvertible`. A short canonical string exists but any paraphrase is equally correct, so exact comparison would penalize correct answers; only an LLM judge or a task change (multiple choice) scores them. In the reviewed set this class is roughly one in six items; see B2 for the exact figure.
- **Vague or hedged gold** (`vague_gold`): "soon after Monday night", "flooring that is not smooth". The information need itself is fuzzy.
- **Ambiguous questions** (`ambiguous_question`): the email supports several distinct answers (e.g. the signer versus the header sender; "Enron" versus "Enron North America Corp.").
- **Gold problems** (`gold_unsupported`, `gold_wrong`): about 1% each in the reviewed set. These items should be excluded or re-labelled rather than converted.
- **Boilerplate targets** (`boilerplate`): questions about list-server footers, disclaimers, or signature blocks. They are convertible but measure nothing about the email's content; a spec may want to down-weight or exclude them.

### 5.3 Evidence validation

For convertible items the canonical answer should be re-validated programmatically against the email (verbatim presence for spans/entities/addresses; token presence for numbers and dates after normalization). Stage 2 already computes these signals for the *gold sentence*; running the same checks on the *canonical answer* is the acceptance gate proposed in §8. Items whose canonical answer cannot be located in the email after normalization should not enter a deterministic test set.

### 5.4 Reviewer uncertainty

The reviewer's own confidence is reported per item (B3). `convertible_rewrite` decisions are mostly medium confidence because the rewrite itself is a judgment call; `unconvertible` decisions split between high and medium. Section 7 quantifies label stability with a second pass.

## 6. Do the supplied `incorrect_answers` help multiple choice?

Multiple choice changes the task from generation to discrimination, so it is a separate evaluation mode, not a proof that an item converted faithfully. On the data itself (100% screening, A4 and `mcq_summary.json`):

- **Hedge leakage:** 10.1% of questions have at least one distractor asserting the information is unavailable ("cannot be determined", "the email does not specify"). Because gold never hedges in those items, the distractor is eliminable without reading the email.
- **Anachronism leakage:** 7.2% of questions have a distractor containing a year after 2002 (the corpus ends in 2002), e.g. "2022-03-01", "suspended in 2011". Eliminable by a date filter.
- **Length/style leakage:** distractors are on median 3 words longer than gold (p90 = +15), and they are frequently more generic. The Fable reviewer flagged trivially distinguishable distractors (`mcq_leak_style`) in about a quarter of reviewed items.
- **Degenerate distractors:** 1.27% of items have two byte-identical distractors; 0.03% have a distractor identical to the gold or alternate answer; the reviewer flagged a further ~0.7% of reviewed items where an "incorrect" answer is actually acceptable given the email.
- **Number reuse:** 6.2% of distractors share gold's numbers, which is good (harder distractors); the rest change the numbers, so a numeric MCQ item is often solvable by locating the number in the email rather than by answering the question.

Conclusion: the supplied distractors can seed an MCQ mode but need filtering (drop hedges, anachronisms, duplicates, near-gold) and probably regeneration to control length and specificity. A 3-option MCQ with these distractors overstates ability. MCQ remains the only deterministic option for the `free_text` class, and it should be reported separately from schema-based exact scoring.

## 8. Proposed acceptance criteria for a reformulated item (not agreed)

An item enters the deterministic test set only if all hold:

1. Verdict `convertible`, or `convertible_rewrite` with the rewritten question stored alongside the original **and** a `narrowed: true|false` marker stating whether the rewrite dropped part of the original information need (dropped-part items are reported separately and never counted as faithful conversions).
2. Canonical answer conforms to its schema's grammar (ISO date, `USD` amount, JSON array/object, etc.) and validates against a schema-specific normalizer.
3. Canonical answer (or one alias) is locatable in the email after normalization; numbers and dates in it occur in the email or in its headers.
4. No `gold_unsupported`, `gold_wrong`, `ambiguous_question`, or `vague_gold` flag.
5. For `list`/`multi_field`, every element/field individually passes 2–3.
6. For items flagged `normalization_risk` or `multi_valid_alias`, an alias list is present and reviewed.
7. Optional policy switches: exclude `boilerplate`; exclude questions whose text is duplicated across more than N emails.

Scoring per schema (proposal): exact match after normalization for scalar schemas; set equality (or ordered equality when flagged) for lists; per-field exact match with all-fields-required for multi-field; verbatim containment for spans. Report accuracy with the explicit denominator (items that passed acceptance) and, separately, the excluded count by reason.

## 10. Limitations

- Stage 3 labels come from one model family at low effort; §7 measures self-consistency, not human agreement. No human item-level review was performed.
- Coverage of stage 3 is exactly what §5 / AGGREGATES §B state. Percentages there describe the reviewed sample; the sample is uniform over emails by construction but is still a sample until coverage reaches 100%.
- Emails longer than 12,000 characters were clipped in the reviewer prompt (flagged in the batch files); items whose evidence lies beyond the clip may be misjudged.
- Two prompt versions were used: the first 99 batches (about 4,000 questions) ran before the `narrowed` marker for question rewrites existed and are counted as "unknown" in AGGREGATES B7; every cached batch records its prompt hash.
- The `include_email` field is undocumented; its meaning was not inferred.
- Stage-2 regexes are deliberately simple; they are screening signals, not labels.
- Licensing of the dataset is unresolved (LICENSING.md); nothing derived from item text is published here beyond the short examples in §9.

## 11. Recommendations for `SPEC.md` (proposals, not agreed)

1. Adopt an explicit per-item **answer schema** field with the categories in §5.1, plus `unconvertible`; ship canonical answers and alias lists only for items passing §8.
2. Treat the deterministic set as a **subset** of EnronQA with a published denominator per split; report excluded counts by reason. Do not claim to score all 528,304 items deterministically.
3. Keep **MCQ** as a separately reported mode with filtered/regenerated distractors; never mix MCQ accuracy with schema-exact accuracy in one number.
4. Store both the **original** and **rewritten** question; default the CLI to the rewritten form for deterministic scoring and expose the original for comparison with the paper's LLM-judge numbers.
5. Implement schema **normalizers** in Go with golden tests (dates with partial precision, currency, lists, aliases) before any scorer.
6. Resolve **licensing** before shipping a derived dataset; prefer runtime download of the pinned revision plus a small published "reformulation overlay" keyed by `split/path/qidx`, if the authors agree.
